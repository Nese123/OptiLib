How OptiLib Works

From target input to optimized library — a fully automated pipeline powered by advanced algorithms.

1
📂

Upload Targets

Upload CSV or Excel files containing your biological targets of interest — by name, ChEMBL ID, Gene Symbol, or UniProt Accession. The system validates each target against the ChEMBL database.

2
🔬

Build Selectivity Matrix

OptiLib queries ChEMBL to find compounds that are both potent and selective against your targets. A compound × target selectivity matrix is constructed, capturing how well each compound distinguishes between targets.

3
💰

Price Estimation

Compound prices are fetched from MolPort's database. For compounds not found, the integrated MolPrice neural network predicts prices from molecular structure — ensuring every candidate has cost data.

4
🧬

Drug Library Optimization

The algorithm simultaneously optimizes two competing objectives: maximizing biological selectivity coverage and minimizing total library cost. It evolves a population of candidate libraries over hundreds of generations to find the best trade-offs, known as the Pareto front.

5
📊

Results Dashboard

Explore the Pareto front interactively, compare optimized libraries against the full compound pool, visualize selectivity heatmaps, and download the winning library — all in a rich, real-time dashboard.

The NSGA-II Algorithm

Understanding the evolutionary engine that powers OptiLib.

🧬 Evolutionary Approach

NSGA-II (Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II) is a widely-used multi-objective optimization algorithm. It maintains a population of candidate solutions (drug libraries) and evolves them through selection, crossover, and mutation — mimicking natural evolution to discover optimal trade-offs.

⚖️ Two Competing Objectives

OptiLib formulates the problem with two objectives that naturally compete: maximize selectivity (you want the best possible target coverage) and minimize cost (you want to spend as little as possible). The algorithm finds the full spectrum of trade-offs between these goals.

📐 Pareto Optimality

Instead of a single "best" answer, NSGA-II produces a Pareto front — a set of solutions where no solution is better than another in both objectives simultaneously. This lets researchers choose the library that best fits their budget and selectivity requirements.

🏗️ Smart Initialization

OptiLib seeds the initial population with intelligently constructed libraries rather than random selections. This accelerates convergence and ensures the algorithm starts from a strong foundation, reaching better solutions faster.
